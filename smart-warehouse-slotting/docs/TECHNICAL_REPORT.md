# Smart Warehouse Slotting & Optimization Engine — Technical Report

This report documents the system architecture, constraint handling, algorithms, computational
complexity, EDA insights, and the baseline-vs-optimized evaluation results for the Smart Warehouse
Slotting problem (PS Track 2).

All numbers below are from the checked-in artifacts in `outputs/`, generated by
`python app/main.py run-all` on the canonical synthetic dataset (`../dataset/processed`, seed 42).

---

## 1. Problem formulation, assumptions, and scope

### Problem
Assign each SKU's real on-hand inventory to physical warehouse locations (bins on racks/shelf
levels) to minimize demand-weighted pick-path travel while respecting spatial, structural weight,
category/hazard, handling, and ergonomic constraints, and prove a measurable improvement over a
feasible unoptimized baseline.

### Decision variables
- `x_ij = 1` if SKU `i` occupies location `j`.
- A SKU may span multiple bins (`Σ_j x_ij >= 1`) but a bin holds at most one SKU type.
- Feasibility gate: `x_ij <= a_ij`, where `a_ij = 1` iff SKU `i` is compatible with location `j`
  under the Phase-4 validator.
- Inventory conservation: for every SKU, `Σ_j assigned_quantity_ij = on_hand_i`. Quantities are
  relocated, never created or discarded.

### Assumptions
- Baseline and optimized layouts are stored separately and never conflated; both hold the same
  inventory.
- `unit_volume_cm3 = width × height × depth`; stored volume/weight = quantity × unit value.
- Zone eligibility is explicit: `allowed_handling` and `allowed_hazard` per location drive
  handling/hazard admissibility (hazardous SKUs only in hazard zones; heavy only in heavy zones;
  fragile excluded from heavy/hazard zones).
- Heavy SKUs are restricted to shelf levels 0–2.
- Routing uses shortest paths over the aisle network in `location_graph.csv` (no straight line
  through racks).
- The dataset is synthetic and provenance-labelled (`data_source` / `data_quality_status`).

### Scope
Phases implemented end-to-end: domain model, dataset + EDA, demand/affinity/velocity analysis,
constraint validator, allocation optimizer, route simulation + evaluation, interactive dashboard,
and integration/documentation.

## 4. Spatial, structural, and safety constraint logic

The Phase-4 validator (`src/validation/validator.py`) is a **hard** gate. Every rule returns a
structured violation with reason, severity, and rule id; `validate` reports all violations, not
just the first.

| Rule | id | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Dimensional fit | SP-01 | `sku.w/h/d <= loc.w/h/d` per dimension |
| Referential integrity | REF-01/02 | SKU and location must exist |
| Hazard/zone compatibility | HZ-01 | `sku.hazard_class` must be in `loc.allowed_hazard` |
| Handling/zone compatibility | HD-01 | `sku.handling_type` must be in `loc.allowed_handling` |
| Heavy shelf height | ER-01 | heavy SKUs prohibited above shelf level 2 |
| Positive quantity | QTY-01 | assigned quantity must be > 0 |
| Single SKU per bin | AS-01 | at most one distinct SKU type per bin |
| Duplicate pin | DU-01 | a SKU cannot be assigned twice to the same bin |
| Assignment completeness | COMP-01 | every SKU with on-hand inventory has a home |
| Inventory conservation | INV-01/02 | assigned total must equal on-hand per SKU |
| Bin load | LD-01 | `Σ quantity × unit_weight <= max_weight_kg` |
| Bin volume | VD-01 | `Σ quantity × unit_volume <= usable_volume_cm3` |
| Shelf structural load | SL-01 | load per `(rack_id, shelf_level) <= shelf_max_weight_kg` |
| Rack structural load | RK-01 | load per rack `<= rack_max_weight_kg` |
| Co-storage compatibility | CP-01 | incompatible category pairs may not share a bin |

Multi-bin placement is supported (one SKU across several bins); the single-SKU rule applies per
bin. The canonical baseline passes all checks: 1,425 assignments, 0 violations, 2,850 rule checks.

## 5. ABC and affinity-analysis methods

### ABC classification (documented Pareto rule)
- Aggregate demand (distinct-order frequency) per SKU over Scenario A, including zero-demand SKUs.
- Sort descending; compute cumulative demand share.
- **Class A** = first 80% of cumulative demand; **Class B** = next 15% (to 95%);
  **Class C** = remainder (including zero-demand SKUs). Thresholds configurable.

Result on the benchmark: **293 SKUs in A** (~24% of SKUs, ~80% of picks), **444 in B**, **463 in C**.

### Order affinity
- Pair statistics over Scenario C: `support, confidence (both directions), lift, co_count`.
- Clusters by connected components on a lift-thresholded (`lift >= 5.0`) and
  co-occurrence-thresholded (`co_count >= 8`) graph → **114 affinity clusters** from 13,062
  candidate pairs.

## 6. Optimization algorithm and objective functions

### Hard vs. soft
- **Hard:** all Phase-4 rules. Never traded away.
- **Soft (objective):** travel cost (D), affinity penalty (A), ergonomics (E), utilization (U).

### Objective
```
minimize  alpha·D + beta·A + gamma·E + delta·U
```
weights (config): `alpha=1.0, beta=0.4, gamma=0.3, delta=0.2`.

- **D (travel)**: demand-weighted distance from each assigned bin to dispatch.
- **A (affinity)**: penalty for separating co-ordered SKU pairs (they should be in nearby bins,
  not co-located, because a bin holds one SKU type).
- **E (ergonomics)**: penalizes high-demand SKUs placed outside the golden shelf zone.
- **U (utilization)**: penalizes under-filled bins.

### Algorithm
1. **Reference layout:** the feasible baseline is the starting point and the fallback.
2. **Greedy feasible starter:** sort SKUs by velocity (fast movers first), place each SKU's real
   on-hand quantity into nearby feasible bins, splitting across bins when a single bin cannot hold
   the whole quantity. The starter tracks bin volume, bin weight, shelf weight, and rack weight.
3. **Simulated-annealing local search:** swap two assignment records' bins or move one record to
   another feasible bin; each candidate is re-validated by the Phase-4 validator. The best feasible
   solution seen is tracked separately from the annealing walk.
4. **Selection:** choose the better feasible candidate (greedy vs. baseline), refine it, then keep
   the refinement only if feasible and no worse; otherwise return the starter. Inventory totals are
   invariant under all moves.

Heuristic results are **not** labeled globally optimal.

### Complexity
- Greedy: `O(n_skus × n_locations)` feasibility checks, with per-SKU feasible-bin caching.
- Local search: `O(max_iterations × (validation + objective))`, dominated by `O(n_assignments)`
  validation/objective passes.
- Validator: one grouped pass per rule over assignments.
- Route simulation: one Dijkstra per source for the pairwise distance matrix, then nearest-neighbor
  per batch.

On the benchmark (1,200 SKUs / 2,304 locations / 1,534 optimized assignments), the optimizer runs
in ~10 s and the whole `run-all` pipeline in well under a minute on a laptop.

## 7. Evaluation methodology and actual results

### Method
- Identical order workload (Scenario A), warehouse geometry, aisle graph, and routing
  (nearest-neighbor, batch size 5) for BOTH layouts.
- Both layouts are validated; violations are reported, not hidden.
- Both layouts hold the same 6,375 inventory units, so cube utilization is inventory-fair and must
  be identical — relocation cannot change total stored volume.

### Metrics (`run-all`, seed 42)

| Metric | Baseline | Optimized | Δ | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total travel distance (m) | 1,707,167.15 | 1,296,259.40 | −410,907.75 | **−24.07%** |
| Avg travel distance (m) | 1,576.33 | 1,196.92 | −379.41 | −24.07% |
| Avg route completion time (s) | 1,370.30 | 1,078.44 | −291.86 | −21.30% |
| Order throughput (/hr) | 13.14 | 16.69 | +3.55 | +27.02% |
| Cube utilization (%) | 15.42 | 15.42 | 0.00 pp | same inventory |
| Constraint violations | 0 | **0** | — | required 0 |
| Assignment feasibility (%) | 100 | **100** | — | required 100 |
| Safety compliant | yes | **yes** | — | — |
| Optimizer runtime (s) | — | ~10 (varies) | — | reported |

Optimizer objective (demand-weighted travel + affinity + ergonomics + utilization):
baseline **2,336,184** → greedy starter **1,698,063** → final **1,670,432**, a **−28.50%**
improvement over the baseline, with 6,375/6,375 units placed and 0 violations.

## 8. EDA insights

From `outputs/eda/eda_report.json` (Scenario A demand, Scenario C affinity):
- Demand is strongly right-skewed: **293 of 1,200 SKUs (~24%) drive ~80% of picks**; 48 SKUs have
  no demand in Scenario A and are classified C.
- The affinity structure shows strong co-purchase pairs: top Scenario-C pairs reach lift > 140
  with 10–11 co-occurrences.
- Median bin usable volume is 108,000 cm³; **94.25%** of SKUs fit that representative bin by
  volume. Space pressure therefore comes mainly from total quantity and weight, not single-unit
  footprint.

## 9. Trade-offs, limitations, and future improvements

### Limitations
- Dataset is synthetic (clearly labelled). Real-world validation requires ingesting a source
  warehouse dataset mapped into the same schema.
- Nearest-neighbor routing is heuristic; S-shape and full TSP are not exhaustively compared.
- SKU dimensioning is bounding-box; packing inefficiency inside a bin is not modelled.
- The heavy shelf-height threshold is fixed (configurable, not tuned).
- Utilization counts bounding-box volume; actual packed volume may differ.
- The optimizer is a heuristic with a fixed iteration budget; `globally_optimal` is explicitly
  false.

### Trade-offs
- Travel cost is weighted against affinity/ergonomics/utilization; the measured −24% travel
  reduction comes with unchanged cube utilization (same inventory), not an artificial gain.
- Greedy + annealing trades search completeness for speed (seconds vs. MILP on this instance).
- One SKU type per bin simplifies feasibility but may under-fill large bins.

### Future improvements
- Add a MILP benchmark on a smaller instance and 2-opt/VRP routing.
- Model multi-pickers, congestion, and seasonality in demand.
- Calibrate objective weights against operational cost data.
- Ingest a real warehouse dataset and re-validate the pipeline unchanged.
